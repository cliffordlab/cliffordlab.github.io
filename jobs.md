We are always seeking talented postdocs and graduate students. Even if you don't see a post open, please do reach out to me.
I can't promise I'll answer every email – it's impossible and I have to focus on the ones that stand out. 
Please send a full CV with a cover letter/email describing your skills and career aspirations to cliffordlab AT jhu DOT edu. 
I'm not looking for a list of awards or publications. One really good publication as lead author (even if it's still under review) is better than four second-author papers or average conference articles. It doesn't even have to be highly novel – it just needs to be really well done. I see very little novely these days. Please be specific about what courses you excelled in, any programming skills, and relevant experience. 

**Please do not use AI to generate your application details or your cover letter**. The vast majority of emails I receive these days are obviously LLM-generated, and these will be rejected immediately. I don't need to know what your LLM thought of one of my random papers, or how it might be extended. I'm looking for passion, openness to new ideas, and original thoughts, even if your email is full of grammatical errors and spelling mistakes. I will delete any emails that appear to be overly AI-generated. Unless you already have a funded fellowship for a specific research topic, please don't include your own research proposal, as my funding is generally for specific topics. 

Please read <a href="https://gdclifford.info/ethics">my ethics page</a>, and let me know if you agree to guidelines there (and why you might not agree with a particular element). I know it seems a little detailed, but almost every item is related to something a student has done (or not done) which I thought was an unspoken rule. Cultures are different across space, time and social groups. I try not to judge and try to be as clear as possible. I encourage diversity in thinking and particularly thinking in different languages.  


### **Open Postdoctoral Fellow Positions**

Postdoctoral Fellow / Research Scientist in Biomedical Machine Learning & Physiological Signal Processing
Departments of Biomedical Engineering / Anesthesiology and Critical Care Medicine | Johns Hopkins University
Location: Baltimore, MD (On-site / Hybrid)

**About the Role**
I'm looking for one or more highly motivated Postdoctoral Fellows to co-lead the development of next-generation, real-time clinical decision-support and predictive monitoring systems, going beyond traditional approaches to AI and clinical informatics.

Our work sits at the intersection of edge computing, high-frequency physiological signal analysis, cutting-edge machine learning, and translational clinical deployment. In this role, you will design, benchmark, and deploy algorithms operating on high-resolution waveform data (e.g., arterial blood pressure, ECG, EEG, plethysmography) and multimodal bedside telemetry to predict acute physiological deterioration and guide clinical interventions in real time across surgical and intensive care units.

Additionally, you will play an active role in designing, organizing, and evaluating datasets and benchmark algorithms for the globally recognized PhysioNet Challenges, contributing directly to open-access scientific discovery and algorithmic reproducibility in digital medicine.

**Key Responsibilities**
Real-Time Predictive Modeling: Design, validate, and optimize state-of-the-art machine learning models (deep learning, sequence models, self-supervised representation learning) for real-time risk stratification and event detection in perioperative and critical care environments.

Physiological Signal Processing: Develop robust filtering, artifact rejection, feature extraction, and waveform decomposition pipelines for continuous, noisy, multi-channel physiological streams.

PhysioNet Challenge Leadership: Co-lead the development of upcoming PhysioNet Challenges, including problem formulation, dataset curation, baseline model implementation, scoring metric development, and scientific dissemination.

Translational Pipeline Integration: Collaborate with clinicians, biomedical engineers, and research software teams to validate models on large-scale multimodal clinical archives (e.g., MIMIC, eICU, and local EHR/waveform repositories) and bridge algorithms into streaming testbeds.

Dissemination: Present novel findings at premier conferences (Computing in Cardiology, NeurIPS, IEEE EMBC, CHIL) and author high-impact peer-reviewed publications.

**Qualifications & Requirements** (some mixture of the follows):
Education: Ph.D. (or near completion) in Biomedical Engineering, Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, Applied Mathematics, Computational Medicine, or a closely related quantitative field.

Machine Learning: Proven experience implementing and tuning modern machine learning approaches for time-series forecasting, anomaly detection, or representation learning.

Biomedical Signal Processing: Theoretical and hands-on understanding of continuous physiological signals (ECG, ABP, PPG, EEG, Respiration, mechanical ventilation parameters), including digital filter design, spectral estimation, and artifact removal.

Software Engineering: Strong proficiency in Python and modern data science / ML stacks (scipy, numpy, scikit-learn, torchaudio/torchvision). Familiarity with containerized workflows (Docker), version control (Git), and reproducible research principles.

Domain Experience: Experience working with large open-access medical databases (e.g., PhysioNet / WFDB toolkits) or streaming clinical ICU/OR data is strongly preferred.

**What We Offer**
Direct immersion in an interdisciplinary environment bridging Johns Hopkins Medicine and the Whiting School of Engineering.

Access to massive multimodal clinical waveform repositories, institutional high-performance computing clusters, and real-time clinical deployment testbeds.

Global visibility and community leadership through deep involvement with the PhysioNet Challenges.

Dedicated mentorship toward independent academic faculty or senior industry R&D careers.

**To Apply**
Please submit the following materials in a single email to cliffordlab at jhh dot edu with the subject line "Application: ML/Signal Processing Postdoc - [Your Full Name]":

A detailed Curriculum Vitae (CV) including a full list of publications and open-source contributions.

A Cover Letter (1–2 pages) summarizing your research background, specific expertise in signal processing / ML, and interest in clinical ML/data science and PhysioNet Challenges.

Links to code repositories (GitHub/GitLab) or relevant project portfolios.

Contact information for three professional references
